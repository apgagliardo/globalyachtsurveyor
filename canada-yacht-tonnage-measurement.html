Canadian Yacht Tonnage Measurement

Planning to register a yacht in Canada? Understand how tonnage measurement fits into the process, what information to gather, and where Transport Canada’s current requirements apply.

Why tonnage matters for Canadian registration

For Canadian vessel registration, tonnage is a regulatory measure determined under the Vessel Registration and Tonnage Regulations and Transport Canada’s Standard for the Tonnage Measurement of Vessels (TP 13430). It is distinct from a vessel’s displacement or weight. The applicable measurement method depends on vessel characteristics and current regulatory requirements.

Transport Canada states that registered vessels must have tonnage determined for registration. Full calculations under the standard are performed by a tonnage measurer appointed by the Minister of Transport.

Simplified measurement and appointed measurement

Some qualifying small vessels may use the owner-completed Simplified Method in Part 3 of TP 13430. The published criteria include a monohull with a tonnage measurement length of 15 metres or less, no more than one tier of deckhouses or superstructures, and combined length of those structures no greater than 70% of tonnage measurement length. Transport Canada also provides a separate form for certain simple multihulls. Eligibility depends on meeting the full standard, so owners should confirm the applicable form and method with Transport Canada.

Other cases require measurement by an appointed tonnage measurer. The standard contains different requirements for vessels below 24 metres and vessels 24 metres or more. This page does not claim an appointment or authorized scope for either length category. Appointment and any service offering remain subject to Transport Canada’s formal confirmation. For information about Global Yacht Surveyor’s established tonnage measurement practice, see international yacht tonnage measurement.

Planning for a yacht outside Canada

Canadian yacht owners frequently purchase, register, and operate vessels outside Canada. Global Yacht Surveyor’s established international practice includes the Mediterranean and Caribbean, with other locations considered by arrangement.

For owners, brokers, and registration agents coordinating Canadian registration overseas, early identification of the applicable measurement requirements can help simplify the process.

Begin by collecting vessel particulars, current registration documents, existing tonnage certificates, general arrangement plans and the receiving registry’s instructions. Vessel location, access to plans, intended use and transaction dates can affect practical arrangements. A Canadian registration requirement does not itself establish that a vessel can be measured remotely or that a particular method will be accepted.

Once appointment status and scope are formally confirmed, availability, location, measurement arrangements and fees can be discussed against the vessel’s circumstances.

Questions from owners, brokers and agents

Can I book a Canadian tonnage measurement now?

No. Appointment is pending and services are in preparation. Training is scheduled for November 19, 2026, and any service depends on formal Transport Canada appointment and confirmed scope.

Can owners use the simplified method?

Owners may use it only when the vessel meets the applicable TP 13430 criteria. The method is optional for qualifying vessels, and it is not a substitute where the rules require an appointed measurer.

Does the 24-metre threshold determine whether a vessel needs measurement?

The standard has separate provisions for vessels below 24 metres and vessels 24 metres or more. The threshold alone does not establish a vessel’s eligibility, required method or any measurer’s appointment scope.

What should an overseas owner send?

For an initial enquiry about future availability, provide the vessel’s make, model, year, principal dimensions, current location, registration status, intended Canadian registration, available plans and target dates.
