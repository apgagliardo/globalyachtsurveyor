Yacht Pre-Purchase Surveys

Engineering Insight. Thorough Investigation. Clear Reporting.

A yacht's condition cannot always be judged by its appearance, maintenance history, or the operation of its equipment. A comprehensive pre-purchase survey should help a buyer understand not only what deficiencies exist, but what those findings may mean for the vessel's safety, reliability, value, and future ownership.

Global Yacht Surveyor combines hands-on marine surveying with a background in naval architecture, yacht construction, and engineering. Our approach emphasizes careful inspection, informed technical judgment, and clear reporting that supports the buyer's purchasing decision.

Every yacht and every assignment is different. The scope of examination is tailored to the vessel, its history, the buyer's particular concerns, and the agreed inspection arrangements.

A Thorough, Engineering-Led Approach

A relatively new production yacht presents different questions from an older cruising vessel that has undergone decades of repairs, modifications, and equipment upgrades.

A survey begins with a systematic examination of the vessel, but observations may warrant further investigation.

For example, a machinery installation may appear professionally executed while raising questions about whether the equipment is appropriately sized for the vessel. Similarly, an apparently minor structural distortion may indicate a previous overload or a more substantial underlying problem.

Recognizing these relationships is an important part of evaluating a yacht.

Where appropriate, the examination may incorporate available design drawings, technical specifications, construction details, maintenance records, and engineering analysis to better understand significant findings.

Our approach combines systematic inspection with engineering analysis and professional judgment, helping buyers understand not only the condition of a vessel but also the significance and potential consequences of material findings.

What a Pre-Purchase Survey Includes

A typical pre-purchase survey examines the vessel's accessible structure, machinery, equipment, and principal operating systems.

Depending on the vessel and agreed scope, this generally includes:

  • Hull and structure: Hull, decks, superstructure, bulkheads, and accessible structural components.
  • Underwater inspection: Underwater hull, keel, rudder, through-hull fittings, and running gear during haul-out.
  • Propulsion systems: Main engines, transmissions, shafting, propellers, fuel systems, exhaust arrangements, and related installations.
  • Electrical systems: Electrical generation, distribution, charging equipment, batteries, and principal wiring installations.
  • Mechanical and domestic systems: Steering, plumbing, sanitation, bilge systems, refrigeration, and other onboard equipment.
  • Navigation and safety: Navigation, communications, fire protection, and safety equipment.
  • Spars and rigging: Visible standing and running rigging, spars, and sailing equipment where applicable.
  • Documentation: Available construction records, maintenance history, equipment information, and technical documentation.
  • Operational assessment: Appropriate equipment checks and sea-trial observations when included in the agreed scope.
  • Condition and value: Evaluation of overall condition and relevant market value.

Additional examinations, including aloft rigging inspections, specialized machinery evaluations, or further technical investigations, may be arranged where appropriate and agreed.

Certain equipment and structures may require dismantling, specialist testing, or access beyond the scope of a standard survey. Such limitations are documented rather than assumed away.

Your Eyes and Ears When You Cannot Be There

Yacht purchases frequently involve buyers who live hundreds or thousands of miles from the vessel they are considering.

When you cannot attend the inspection personally, the surveyor's role becomes particularly important.

Global Yacht Surveyor places considerable emphasis on detailed photographic documentation, clear explanations, and reporting that helps the buyer develop an informed understanding of the vessel.

Photographs are used not only to document deficiencies, but also to illustrate equipment arrangements, construction details, general condition, and other characteristics that may be important to the prospective owner.

The objective is not simply to deliver a list of deficiencies. It is to communicate what was examined, what was found, why significant findings matter, and what questions remain unresolved.

A useful survey report should help you understand the yacht as a whole, including its strengths, weaknesses, maintenance needs, and potential future liabilities.

Understanding the Findings

Not every deficiency has the same significance.

A minor maintenance item, a system nearing the end of its service life, and a structural concern requiring further investigation have very different implications for a buyer.

Our reporting distinguishes significant safety and condition findings from routine maintenance observations and identifies matters requiring specialist examination or further verification.

Where the available evidence supports it, findings are discussed in the context of their likely consequences for vessel operation, reliability, maintenance, and ownership.

The purpose is to provide useful technical information for your purchasing decision, not simply a lengthy inventory of equipment.
